## Policy, Regulation & Macroeconomics

**The White House Redefines Economic Growth: AI as the New Industrial Revolution Engine**
The White House Council of Economic Advisers has positioned artificial intelligence as the dominant engine of U.S. economic growth, contributing 1.3% to national GDP in early 2025 alone. Framing AI as the central macroeconomic driver since industrialization, the Council's thesis invokes the “Great Divergence”—arguing that nations investing most aggressively in compute, talent, and innovation will establish compounding global advantages. The report's economic forecasts point to productivity growth scenarios ranging from single-digit gains to more than 45% GDP expansion within a decade, justified further by flagship policies such as the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and trade agreements favoring U.S. AI chip exports.

For AI industry leaders, this new doctrine advances critical implications: capital intensity and compute access now overtake algorithmic breakthroughs as key differentiators, tilting competitive advantage toward well-funded incumbents and sovereign-backed enterprises. The administration’s priority on deregulation and infrastructure investment foreshadows rapid AI scaling, with decreased friction costs and an explicit push to outpace regulatory caution—a marked pivot from prior risk-management-focused narratives. Internationally, the framework legitimizes tech export controls designed to maintain U.S. dominance over China and frames the EU’s regulatory stance as competitively limiting. With “AI determinism” driving policy, U.S. CEOs and investors can expect structural tailwinds accelerating deployment across industries.

## Company Strategies & Partnerships

**Starlink Disrupts Data Landscape: Privacy Policy Update Powers Proprietary AI Training**
Starlink’s January 2026 privacy update redefines SpaceX’s positioning, unlocking vast anonymized user datasets—spanning usage, locations, and global behavior—for proprietary AI model training. This enables Starlink to leverage petabytes of telemetry for machine learning advances in network optimization, predictive maintenance, and service personalization. The policy explicitly permits anonymized data use for AI—both internally and via select third parties, with opt-out controls for users regarding external AI access. Retention extends two years beyond account closure, creating a rich, orbital-scale dataset unmatched by terrestrial ISPs. Encryption and stringent internal safeguards protect user data, though only Starlink retains unqualified AI rights.

Strategically, this cements Starlink’s dual identity as a telecom and AI powerhouse, with its 4M+ user base feeding a proprietary moat on par with Big Tech. AI-optimized networks could slash costs across its 6,000+ satellite fleet and open major enterprise and mobility revenue. Limited third-party access incentivizes edge AI collaborations, fueling an ecosystem while maintaining CCPA compliance. The move intensifies pressures on competitors like OneWeb and Kuiper, as no rival can yet match Starlink’s global, granular data. Downstream, expect synergistic plays with xAI and Tesla, channeling insights into autonomous and multimodal AI systems. Regulatory scrutiny and Q2 2026 model unveilings will test this bold new data-for-AI paradigm.

**OpenAI’s $100B Nvidia Megadeal Falters, Shifting Competitive Dynamics**
OpenAI’s ambitious $100 billion investment pact with Nvidia has stalled, revealing vulnerabilities in its capital-intensive growth strategy. With voracious GPU requirements to power next-gen models, OpenAI’s planned data centers would have drawn power rivaling that of New York City. However, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang expressed skepticism over the deal’s non-binding nature and OpenAI’s operational discipline. This bottleneck exposes OpenAI to shifting power dynamics: Amazon and SoftBank are in talks for multi-billion-dollar stakes, but Nvidia—the decade-long partner—may now prioritize third-party infrastructure providers like CoreWeave.

Financially, OpenAI faces mounting losses (projecting $14B in 2026 alone) and IPO preparation pressures, while Amazon could unseat Microsoft as OpenAI’s chief backer. Market-wide, the impasse amplifies barriers to entry, with incumbent chip makers and cloud vendors consolidating control and regulatory risks rising as the EU AI Act nears enforcement. Watch for Amazon’s possible pivot or renegotiations in Q2 2026—a sustained freeze could enable leaner competitors to capitalize amid an industry-wide reevaluation of scale vs. efficiency.

**OpenAI Partners with Cerebras: $10B Bet on Ultra-Low-Latency Inference**
OpenAI’s $10 billion partnership with Cerebras signals a leap in AI inference, deploying 750 megawatts of real-time compute through 2028. Cerebras’ wafer-scale architecture delivers up to 15× faster responses for agentic workloads—like coding assistance and voice or image generation—compared to GPU clusters, with GPT-OSS-120B benchmarks showing 2,700 tokens/sec and 280ms first-token latency. The arrangement diversifies OpenAI’s hardware stack amid Nvidia shortages, strengthening both performance and supply resilience.

Cerebras, a 2015 cofounder alongside OpenAI, lands its largest client, accelerating a path toward a possible $22B IPO. For OpenAI, the integration enhances model responsiveness—a crucial feature for next-gen AI agents and consumer engagement—while establishing compute independence outside the traditional GPU ecosystem. The partnership’s success will hinge on achieving sustained throughput at enterprise and consumer scale, directly pressuring Groq, Nvidia, and cloud hyperscalers to catch up on latency and throughput economics.

## Research & Product Launches

**Physical Intelligence Raises $1B+ to Build Generalist Robot Brains**
Physical Intelligence, founded by Stripe’s Lachy Groom, Google DeepMind alum Quan Vuong, and Berkeley’s Sergey Levine, has secured over $1 billion at a $5.6 billion valuation from Sequoia, Khosla Ventures, and Thrive Capital. Their approach centers on developing foundation models for robotics—allowing low-cost arms (as cheap as $1,000) to perform complex manipulations by transferring learning across platforms without retraining. This cross-embodiment learning slashes deployment costs, with “general motion intuition” built from real-world physical training—contrasting internet-trained vision-language models.

Business-wise, Physical Intelligence is pursuing a high-risk, high-reward approach: focusing solely on foundational breakthroughs, with no immediate revenue plan, in hopes of licensing to OEMs. This strategy mirrors pre-ChatGPT OpenAI investments, aiming to monopolize the emerging “robot brain” market. The company’s rapid R&D cadence—exceeding its initial 5-10 year timeline in just 18 months—underscores a research-first culture competing against rivals like Skild AI ($14B valuation), which targets fast commercialization. The ultimate industry implication is clear: software-driven robot brains will increasingly commoditize hardware, making third-party integrations and model licensing the next battleground for industrial automation.

**Anthropic Unleashes Agentic Plug-Ins for Claude, Turbocharging Enterprise Automation**
Anthropic has escalated enterprise AI automation by launching agentic plug-ins for Claude’s Cowork platform—transforming Claude from a conversational assistant to a customizable departmental coworker. As a macOS research preview, Cowork grants Claude direct, permissioned access to local files, enabling autonomous file management, content conversion, and long-running, multi-agent workflows. Plug-ins bundle role-driven skills, connectors, and sub-agents, with 11 in-house examples open-sourced for marketing, legal, sales, biosciences, and product management.

Users on the Max tier ($100–200/month) can now build, deploy, and share custom workflows, driving significant enterprise productivity and potentially locking teams into Anthropic’s ontology. Open-sourcing catalyzes ecosystem growth, while Claude Code’s rapid path—from preview to billion-dollar revenue in six months—signals enormous scaling upside in the $200B+ knowledge automation market. However, Apple exclusivity and residual security risks demand careful adoption. Overall, this sets a new pace for “coworker” AI, leapfrogging rivals on granular desktop agency and expanding Claude’s competitive lead in agentic workflows for enterprise knowledge work.

## Industrial AI

**PepsiCo, Siemens, and NVIDIA Pioneer Physics-Based Digital Twins—Boosting Throughput & Slashing Costs**
PepsiCo’s joint project with Siemens and NVIDIA is revolutionizing industrial AI through large-scale deployment of physics-based digital twins—achieving 20% throughput gains and reducing capital expenditures by up to 15% at a U.S. Gatorade plant in just three months. Using Siemens Digital Twin Composer and NVIDIA Omniverse, PepsiCo creates high-fidelity, 3D operational replicas, integrating real-time machine and workflow data for near-100% design validation before implementation.

AI agents simulate thousands of configurations to identify bottlenecks, enabling rapid, safe, and capital-efficient plant optimization. Announced at CES 2026, this initiative sets performance benchmarks for global rollouts amid surging CPG demand. The digital twin approach future-proofs PepsiCo’s operations, with measured ROI easily translating into higher margin and agility than rivals like Coca-Cola and Unilever. For Siemens and NVIDIA, it validates Omniverse as the industrial metaverse standard, pressuring analytics incumbents to accelerate AI-powered physical simulation. This shift accelerates manufacturing’s transition toward self-optimizing, agent-driven systems—a critical trend as factories race to realize 90%+ preemptive issue detection and compress upgrade cycles.
